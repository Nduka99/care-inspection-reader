SYNTHETIC example home care agency: Safe

SYNTHETIC: an invented report for a worked example. No real service is described.

Safe

Requires improvement

The rating for this key question went down to requires improvement. Rotas gave carers no time to get from one call to the next, and this broke the staffing regulation.

Safe and effective staffing

Score: 1

The provider did not plan rotas with any travel time between calls. Records for the four weeks before our visit showed that almost a third of morning calls started more than 45 minutes after the agreed time, and 19 calls lasted less than half of their planned length.

One relative told us, "Mum waits in bed until nearly eleven some days, and nobody rings to say they are running late." Two new carers had started visiting people on their own before their references and criminal record checks had come back.

Medicines optimisation

Score: 2

People mostly received their medicines as prescribed, and carers completed the electronic record at each call. However, one person's blood thinner was prescribed for 6pm and was given at any time between 4pm and 9pm, because their teatime call had no fixed time.

Carers applied creams, but three people had no body map showing where each cream should go. The office looked at the records every week and had noticed some of these gaps, but had not acted on them.

Involving people to manage risks

Score: 2

Carers knew people well and could tell us how each person liked to be helped. Plans for people living with diabetes set out the signs of a high or a low blood sugar and what carers should do.

A person who had fallen twice in the autumn still had a moving plan written before the falls. It did not say that they now needed two carers to help them stand.

Learning culture

Score: 3

Carers logged accidents and near misses on their phones, and the registered manager read each one within two working days. Lessons were talked through at team meetings and in a monthly newsletter.

One form from August recorded a fall but not what was done about it afterwards; the manager added the missing actions while we were there. People and their families felt able to raise a worry and said the office listened.

Infection prevention and control

Score: 4

Carers always had gloves, aprons and hand gel, and spot checks found they used them well at every call we watched. Two carers trained as infection leads visited each new starter on their first shifts to check their practice.

When a stomach bug spread through one village last winter, the agency kept the same small team on the homes affected until it had passed, and nobody else became unwell.
